## 1. The Mobile System Design Interview

### Q1. What is a mobile system design interview, and how does it differ from a backend one? `[Fundamental]`
**Key points:**
- You design a feature or app **end to end**, focusing on the **client**: architecture, data flow, networking, caching, offline behavior, performance, and the API contract with the backend.
- Backend interviews focus on servers, databases, sharding, and load balancing. Mobile interviews focus on **constraints unique to devices**:
  - Unreliable, slow, or expensive networks.
  - Limited battery, memory, storage, and CPU.
  - App lifecycle (suspension, termination) and OS background limits.
  - Slow release cycles (App Store review) and **old app versions in the wild**.
  - Privacy, permissions, and platform guidelines.
- You're still expected to sketch the backend at a high level and to design the API the app needs.

### Q2. How should you structure your answer? `[Fundamental]`
**Key points:** A reliable framework (often remembered as **RADIO**: Requirements, Architecture, Data model, Interface, Optimizations):

| Step | Time (of ~45 min) | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Requirements** | 5–8 min | Clarify functional and non-functional requirements, scope, and scale |
| 2. **High-level architecture** | 8–10 min | Draw client components and how they talk to the backend |
| 3. **Data model** | 5 min | Key entities, local storage schema, caching |
| 4. **API / interface** | 5–8 min | Endpoints, real-time channels, payloads, pagination |
| 5. **Deep dives & optimizations** | 15 min | Offline, sync, performance, security, edge cases, trade-offs |
| 6. **Wrap-up** | 2–3 min | Summarize, mention monitoring, testing, and what you'd do next |

- Let the interviewer steer deep dives, but proactively offer topics: "I could go deeper into offline sync or into image loading. Which is more interesting to you?"

### Q3. What clarifying questions should you ask? `[Fundamental]`
**Key points:**
- **Scope**: Which features are in scope? (e.g. "For a chat app: 1:1 only or groups? media? voice/video?")
- **Users and scale**: How many users? DAU? Global? Expected data volume per user?
- **Platforms**: iPhone only, iPad, Mac, watchOS? Minimum iOS version?
- **Offline**: Must it work offline? Read-only or read-write?
- **Real-time**: How fresh must data be (seconds, minutes)?
- **Constraints**: Low-end devices or emerging markets (data costs)? Accessibility, localization?
- **Security/compliance**: Payments, health data, end-to-end encryption?
- **Existing systems**: Is there a backend already, or do we design the API too?
- Write the answers down as a short requirements list on the whiteboard; refer back to it.

### Q4. What do interviewers evaluate at Senior vs Lead level? `[Senior]`
| Senior | Lead / Staff |
|---|---|
| Solid client architecture with clear components | Same, plus cross-team and cross-platform implications |
| Correct handling of networking, caching, offline, lifecycle | Explicit trade-offs with business impact |
| Good API design for the app's needs | Influences backend/API contracts, versioning strategy |
| Performance and memory awareness | Observability, rollout, experimentation, migration strategy |
| Identifies edge cases | Prioritizes and phases delivery (MVP → v2), risk management |
| Communicates clearly | Drives the conversation, aligns stakeholders, mentors through the design |

### Q6. How deep should you go into the backend? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- Enough to make the client design coherent: API endpoints, real-time mechanism, CDN for media, push notification service, and major data flows.
- Don't spend 20 minutes on database sharding unless asked. Say: "On the backend, I'd assume a feed service with fan-out and a CDN for media; I'll focus on the client unless you'd like me to go deeper."
- Lead candidates should comfortably discuss **API contracts**, BFFs, and how backend choices affect the client (pagination style, consistency guarantees, payload size).

### Q7. How do you communicate trade-offs well? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- State options, criteria, and your choice: "For real-time updates we could poll, use SSE, or WebSockets. Since we need bidirectional, low-latency messaging, I'd use WebSockets, with push notifications when the app is in the background. The cost is connection management and battery; I'd mitigate by disconnecting when backgrounded."
- Tie decisions to **requirements** you clarified earlier.
- Acknowledge downsides and how you'd monitor or mitigate them.

### Q8. Which mobile-specific constraints should you always mention? `[Fundamental]`
**Checklist:**
- **Network**: offline, flaky, high latency, metered/Low Data Mode, captive portals.
- **Lifecycle**: app can be suspended or killed at any time; background execution is limited and not guaranteed.
- **Resources**: battery, memory (jetsam terminations), storage, CPU/thermal limits on older devices.
- **Distribution**: App Store review delays; users on old versions for months; can't hotfix instantly.
- **Platform**: permissions (notifications, location, photos), privacy rules, background modes.
- **UX**: perceived performance (time to first content), accessibility, localization, multiple screen sizes.

## 2. Requirements & Estimation

### Q9. How do you separate functional and non-functional requirements? `[Fundamental]`
**Example: a photo-sharing feed.**
- **Functional**: view a feed of posts; like and comment; pull to refresh; infinite scroll; post a photo with a caption; view a profile.
- **Non-functional**: time to first content under 1 second on a warm start; smooth 60/120 fps scrolling; usable offline (read cached feed, queue likes); data-efficient on cellular; secure auth; accessible and localized; crash-free rate above 99.8%.
- **Out of scope** (say explicitly): stories, direct messages, video editing, ads.

### Q10. What non-functional requirements matter most for mobile? `[Senior]`
| Requirement | Questions to consider |
|---|---|
| **Performance** | Launch time, time to first content, scroll smoothness, memory footprint |
| **Offline / resilience** | What works offline? How are writes queued and synced? |
| **Freshness / consistency** | How stale can data be? Real-time or eventual? |
| **Battery & data usage** | Polling frequency, background work, media sizes, prefetching |
| **Security & privacy** | Auth, token storage, encryption, PII handling, compliance |
| **Scalability (client)** | Large lists, large local databases, many media items |
| **Observability** | Crash reporting, metrics, logging, analytics |
| **Accessibility & i18n** | Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, RTL, localization |
| **Maintainability** | Modular architecture, testability, team ownership |

### Q11. How do you do back-of-the-envelope estimation for a mobile app? `[Senior]`
**Example: feed data usage per session**
```
Feed page:        20 posts × ~1.5 KB JSON          ≈ 30 KB (≈ 8 KB gzipped)
Thumbnails:       20 images × ~60 KB (WebP/HEIC, sized for device) ≈ 1.2 MB
Typical session:  5 pages scrolled                 ≈ 6 MB images + 40 KB JSON
Daily (3 sessions):                                ≈ 18 MB/day
Monthly:                                           ≈ 540 MB/month
```
**Talking points from the numbers:**
- **Images dominate** data usage, so device-appropriate sizes, modern formats (HEIC/WebP/AVIF), and CDN resizing matter far more than JSON optimizations.
- In Low Data Mode, serve lower-quality images and disable video autoplay/prefetching.
- Disk cache budget (e.g. 200 MB for images) with LRU eviction keeps storage bounded.

### Q12. How do you estimate local storage needs? `[Senior]`
**Example: chat app**
```
Text message:        ~200 bytes (with metadata and indexes)
Active user:         ~500 messages/day → 100 KB/day → ~36 MB/year of text
Media:               thumbnails stored; full-size media downloaded on demand and evictable
Plan:                keep all text locally; cap media cache (e.g. 1 GB, user-configurable);
                     provide "manage storage" settings; stream or re-download old media
```
- Mention indexes (conversation ID + sequence) and periodic cleanup of deleted or expired content.

### Q13. Which success metrics would you define? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **Performance**: time to first content (TTFC), app launch time, hang rate, scroll hitch rate.
- **Reliability**: crash-free sessions/users, API error rates, sync failure rates, message delivery latency.
- **Engagement / business**: feature adoption, session length, conversion (checkout), retention.
- **Efficiency**: data usage per session, battery impact, cache hit rate.
- Mentioning metrics early shows you design for **observable outcomes**, not just code structure.

## 3. API Design & Communication Protocols

### Q14. Compare REST, GraphQL, and gRPC for mobile clients. `[Senior]`
| | REST (JSON) | GraphQL | gRPC (Protobuf) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data fetching | Fixed responses per endpoint; may over/under-fetch | Client selects exactly the fields it needs; one request for nested data | Typed RPC methods; compact binary payloads |
| Caching | Excellent HTTP caching (ETag, CDN) | Harder (POST, single endpoint); needs normalized client cache | No HTTP caching by default |
| Tooling on iOS | `URLSession`, `Codable` | Apollo iOS, code generation | grpc-swift, code generation |
| Versioning | URL/header versions | Schema evolution, field deprecation | Protobuf field numbers (backward compatible) |
| Best for | Most apps, CDN-cacheable content | Complex, nested UIs with many screens; multiple clients | High-performance internal APIs, streaming |

- Senior answer: REST is the pragmatic default; GraphQL shines when many screens need different shapes of the same data; gRPC for performance-critical or streaming use cases. Many companies use a **BFF** (Q17) to tailor REST responses for mobile instead.

### Q15. Which real-time mechanism would you choose? `[Senior]`
| Mechanism | How it works | Pros | Cons | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Short polling** | Request every N seconds | Simple, works everywhere | Wasteful, delayed, battery drain | Infrequent updates (order status every 30s) |
| **Long polling** | Server holds the request until data is available | Near real-time over plain HTTP | Connection churn, server resources | Fallback when WebSockets are blocked |
| **Server-Sent Events (SSE)** | One-way stream over HTTP | Simple, auto-reconnect semantics | Server → client only | Live scores, notifications feed, LLM token streaming |
| **WebSockets** | Persistent bidirectional connection | Low latency, bidirectional | Connection management, scaling, battery | Chat, collaboration, live location |
| **Push notifications (APNs)** | Apple delivers to the device | Works when the app is backgrounded/terminated | Not guaranteed, size-limited, rate-limited | Wake/alert users, trigger syncs |

- Typical mobile design: **WebSocket while in the foreground + APNs when in the background + delta sync on reconnect** (so nothing is missed).

### Q16. What makes an API mobile-friendly? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **Small payloads**: only fields the screen needs; compression (gzip/brotli); avoid deeply nested duplicates.
- **Fewer round trips**: aggregate data for a screen into one call (latency dominates on mobile); support batch requests.
- **Pagination** with cursors; **delta sync** endpoints (`changes?since=token`).
- **Image URLs with size parameters** (or multiple renditions) so clients fetch device-appropriate sizes.
- **Idempotency** for writes (Q19); **consistent error format** (Q20).
- **Backward compatibility**: additive changes only; old app versions keep working.
- **Server-provided display strings** where localization or business rules change often (with caution).

### Q17. What is the Backend for Frontend (BFF) pattern? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- A server layer dedicated to a specific client (iOS/Android/web) that **aggregates** calls to many microservices and **shapes** responses for that client's screens.
- Benefits: fewer round trips, smaller payloads, client-specific logic moved server-side (faster to change than shipping app updates), and isolation of clients from backend refactors.
- Costs: another service to own and scale; risk of duplicating business logic across BFFs.

```
iOS app ──▶ Mobile BFF ──┬──▶ User service
                         ├──▶ Feed service
                         ├──▶ Ads service
                         └──▶ Media service
```

### Q18. How do you version APIs when old app versions stay installed for months? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Key points:**
- Assume **many versions in production at once** (users don't update immediately; auto-update is not universal).
- Prefer **additive, backward-compatible changes**: new optional fields, new endpoints; never change field meaning or remove fields that old clients use.
- Clients must **tolerate unknown fields and enum values** (decode unknowns as `.unknown`).
- Send `X-App-Version` and `X-Platform` headers so the backend can tailor or block responses.
- For breaking changes: version endpoints (`/v2/`), support old versions for a defined window, and use a **minimum supported version** / force-update mechanism (Q96) as a last resort.
- Track version adoption in analytics to know when it's safe to remove old paths.

### Q19. What are idempotency keys, and why do mobile apps need them? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- Mobile networks often fail **after** the server processed a request but **before** the client received the response. Retrying a non-idempotent request (create order, send money, post message) could duplicate it.
- The client generates a unique key per logical operation and sends it with every retry; the server stores the result for that key and returns the same response for duplicates.

```swift
struct CreateOrderRequest: Encodable {
    let items: [LineItemDTO]
    let paymentMethodID: String
}

func placeOrder(_ request: CreateOrderRequest, idempotencyKey: UUID) async throws -> OrderDTO {
    var urlRequest = URLRequest(url: baseURL.appending(path: "orders"))
    urlRequest.httpMethod = "POST"
    urlRequest.setValue(idempotencyKey.uuidString, forHTTPHeaderField: "Idempotency-Key")
    urlRequest.httpBody = try JSONEncoder().encode(request)
    urlRequest.setValue("application/json", forHTTPHeaderField: "Content-Type")

    // Retries reuse the SAME key, so the server processes the order at most once
    return try await withRetry(maxAttempts: 3) {
        try await client.send(urlRequest, as: OrderDTO.self)
    }
}

// The key is created once per user action (e.g. when "Place order" is tapped) and persisted
// with the pending operation, so even a retry after an app restart reuses it.
```

### Q20. How should API errors be designed for mobile clients? `[Senior]`
```json
{
  "error": {
    "code": "PAYMENT_DECLINED",
    "message": "Your card was declined.",
    "retryable": false,
    "retry_after_seconds": null,
    "request_id": "req_8f2a91c",
    "details": { "decline_reason": "insufficient_funds" }
  }
}
```
**Key points:**
- A **stable machine-readable code** for client logic (not just HTTP status or English text).
- An optional **user-displayable message** (localized server-side) and a fallback on the client.
- **Retry guidance** (`retryable`, `Retry-After`) so the client knows whether to retry.
- A **request ID** for support and tracing.
- Consistent status codes: 4xx client errors (don't retry, except 408/429), 5xx server errors (retry with backoff).

### Q21. How should the client retry with backoff? `[Senior]`
```swift
struct RetryPolicy {
    var maxAttempts = 4
    var baseDelay: Double = 0.5        // seconds
    var maxDelay: Double = 20

    /// Exponential backoff with "full jitter" to avoid synchronized retry storms
    func delay(forAttempt attempt: Int) -> Duration {
        let exponential = min(maxDelay, baseDelay * pow(2, Double(attempt - 1)))
        return .seconds(Double.random(in: 0...exponential))
    }

    func shouldRetry(_ error: Error) -> Bool {
        if let urlError = error as? URLError {
            return [.timedOut, .networkConnectionLost, .notConnectedToInternet, .cannotConnectToHost]
                .contains(urlError.code)
        }
        if case APIError.httpStatus(let code, _) = error {
            return code == 408 || code == 429 || (500...599).contains(code)
        }
        return false
    }
}
```
**Talking points:**
- **Jitter** matters at scale: after an outage, millions of clients retrying at the same exponential intervals can overload the recovering backend (a "thundering herd").
- Respect `Retry-After` for 429/503.
- Only retry **idempotent** requests (or requests with idempotency keys).
- Combine with a **circuit breaker** for repeatedly failing endpoints (stop calling for a while, show degraded UI).

### Q22. How do you prioritize and cancel network requests? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **Priorities**: user-visible content first (current screen) > prefetch > analytics/background uploads. `URLSessionTask.priority`, separate sessions/queues, and limited concurrency for low-priority work.
- **Cancellation**: cancel requests for screens the user left or cells scrolled away (structured concurrency, `.task` in SwiftUI, cancelling tasks in `prepareForReuse`).
- **Coalescing**: deduplicate identical in-flight requests (e.g. the same avatar URL requested by 10 cells).

### Q23. How do HTTP/2, HTTP/3, and compression help mobile apps? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **HTTP/2**: multiplexes many requests over one TCP connection (no per-request connection setup), header compression. `URLSession` uses it automatically when the server supports it.
- **HTTP/3 (QUIC over UDP)**: faster connection setup, no TCP head-of-line blocking, better behavior on lossy networks and when switching between Wi-Fi and cellular. Supported by `URLSession` (iOS 15+) when servers advertise it.
- **Compression**: gzip/brotli for JSON responses (handled transparently by `URLSession`); compress large request bodies (e.g. analytics batches) explicitly if the server supports it.
- **Connection reuse**: share one `URLSession` instead of creating sessions per request.

### Q24. JSON or Protocol Buffers? `[Senior]`
| JSON | Protocol Buffers |
|---|---|
| Human-readable, easy debugging | Binary, smaller and faster to parse |
| `Codable` built in | Requires code generation (swift-protobuf) |
| Flexible, schema-less | Strict schema, safe evolution via field numbers |
| Larger payloads (mitigated by gzip) | Ideal for high-volume or real-time messaging |

- Common choice: JSON for most APIs, Protobuf for chat/real-time or very high-volume endpoints.

### Q25. How do you design for flaky networks? `[Senior]`
```swift
import Network

@MainActor
@Observable
final class ConnectivityMonitor {
    private(set) var isConnected = true
    private(set) var isExpensive = false      // cellular or personal hotspot
    private(set) var isConstrained = false    // Low Data Mode enabled

    private let monitor = NWPathMonitor()

    init() {
        monitor.pathUpdateHandler = { [weak self] path in
            Task { @MainActor in
                self?.isConnected = path.status == .satisfied
                self?.isExpensive = path.isExpensive
                self?.isConstrained = path.isConstrained
            }
        }
        monitor.start(queue: DispatchQueue(label: "connectivity"))
    }
}
```
**Talking points:**
- Use connectivity as a **hint** (show an offline banner, pause prefetching, trigger sync when connectivity returns), but **never** as a precondition for sending requests. Just try, and handle failures.
- Sensible timeouts (`timeoutIntervalForRequest`), `waitsForConnectivity = true` for non-urgent requests, retries with backoff.
- Respect **Low Data Mode** (`allowsConstrainedNetworkAccess = false` for non-essential requests such as prefetching and high-resolution media).
- Show **cached content** with a "last updated" indicator instead of empty error screens.

## 4. Pagination & Data Loading

### Q26. Compare offset, cursor, and keyset pagination. `[Senior]`
| | Offset (`?page=3&limit=20`) | Cursor (`?cursor=abc&limit=20`) | Keyset (`?before_id=123`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stability when items are inserted/deleted | ❌ Duplicates or skipped items | ✅ Stable | ✅ Stable |
| Jump to page N | ✅ Easy | ❌ Sequential only | ❌ Sequential only |
| Server performance on deep pages | ❌ Slow (`OFFSET` scans) | ✅ Fast | ✅ Fast |
| Best for | Admin tables, small static lists | Feeds, timelines, chat history | Simple ordered data |

```json
{
  "items": [ { "id": "p_981", "author": "…", "created_at": "2026-09-30T10:15:00Z" } ],
  "next_cursor": "eyJjcmVhdGVkX2F0IjoiMjAyNi0wOS0zMFQxMDoxNTowMFoiLCJpZCI6InBfOTgxIn0",
  "has_more": true
}
```
- The cursor is **opaque** to the client (it may encode a timestamp + ID); the server can change its format freely.

### Q27. How do you implement smooth infinite scrolling? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- Trigger loading **before** the end (e.g. when the item 5–10 from the end appears) so the next page arrives before the user reaches the bottom.
- Prevent duplicate requests (`isLoadingMore` flag), deduplicate items by ID, and stop when `has_more` is false.
- Show a footer loading indicator and an inline **retry** cell on failure (don't lose loaded content).
- Prefetch images for upcoming rows; cancel prefetches when the user scrolls the other way.
- Cap in-memory items for extremely long sessions (window older items out and reload them from the local store when scrolling back).
- (Code example: Architecture file, Q98.)

### Q28. How do you handle new items appearing at the top of a feed? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- Don't insert new posts above the user's current reading position automatically (it causes jumpy scrolling). Show a **"New posts" pill**; when tapped, scroll to top and insert.
- Fetch "newer than the first item" with a `before`/`after` cursor pair; if too many new items exist (a large **gap**), reset the feed instead of loading hundreds of items.
- Preserve scroll position on refresh by anchoring to a visible item's ID.

### Q29. What is delta sync? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- Instead of re-downloading everything, the client asks: **"what changed since my last sync token?"** The server returns created, updated, and deleted items plus a new token.

```json
GET /v1/notes/changes?since=st_1042

{
  "changes": [
    { "type": "upsert", "note": { "id": "n_17", "title": "Groceries", "updated_at": "…", "version": 8 } },
    { "type": "delete", "id": "n_09" }
  ],
  "next_token": "st_1057",
  "has_more": false
}
```
- Benefits: small payloads, fast syncs, battery-friendly. The server must retain change history (or tombstones) long enough; if the client's token is too old, the server responds with "full resync required."

### Q30. How do you minimize time to first content? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **Render cached content immediately** on launch (from the local database), then refresh in the background (stale-while-revalidate).
- Make the first screen's API **one aggregated request**; prioritize it over everything else at launch.
- Show **skeleton placeholders** sized like real content (avoids layout jumps); use blur-hash or dominant-color placeholders for images.
- Defer non-critical work (analytics setup, prefetching, SDK initialization) until after the first frame.
- Measure TTFC with signposts and track it in production.

## 5. Caching

### Q31. What caching layers exist in a mobile app? `[Senior]`
```
Request for data
   │
   ├─▶ 1. In-memory cache (NSCache / dictionaries in actors)   ~ nanoseconds, lost on termination
   ├─▶ 2. Local database (SQLite / SwiftData / Core Data)      ~ milliseconds, structured, queryable
   ├─▶ 3. Disk file cache (images, media, JSON blobs)          ~ milliseconds, evictable
   ├─▶ 4. HTTP cache (URLCache, ETag / Cache-Control)          automatic for cacheable responses
   └─▶ 5. Network → CDN → backend                              ~ 100 ms – seconds
```
- Each layer has different **lifetime, capacity, and consistency** characteristics. Mention which layer each data type uses (e.g. feed items in the database, decoded images in memory, image files on disk).

### Q32. How does HTTP caching work, and how do you configure it on iOS? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- `Cache-Control: max-age=300` lets the client reuse a response for 5 minutes without contacting the server.
- `ETag` / `Last-Modified` enable **conditional requests**: the client sends `If-None-Match`, and the server responds `304 Not Modified` with no body if nothing changed (saves bandwidth, still costs a round trip).
- `URLSession` honors these headers automatically through `URLCache`.

```swift
let cache = URLCache(memoryCapacity: 20 * 1024 * 1024,     // 20 MB
                     diskCapacity: 150 * 1024 * 1024)      // 150 MB
let configuration = URLSessionConfiguration.default
configuration.urlCache = cache
configuration.requestCachePolicy = .useProtocolCachePolicy  // follow server cache headers
let session = URLSession(configuration: configuration)
```
- `URLCache` won't store very large responses (relative to its capacity), and per-user API responses should usually be `private`/`no-store` on shared devices.

### Q33. How do you implement conditional requests manually? `[Senior]`
```swift
actor ConditionalFetcher {
    private struct Entry { let etag: String; let data: Data }
    private var entries: [URL: Entry] = [:]
    private let session: URLSession

    init(session: URLSession) { self.session = session }

    func data(from url: URL) async throws -> Data {
        var request = URLRequest(url: url)
        request.cachePolicy = .reloadIgnoringLocalCacheData      // we manage validation ourselves
        if let entry = entries[url] {
            request.setValue(entry.etag, forHTTPHeaderField: "If-None-Match")
        }

        let (data, response) = try await session.data(for: request)
        guard let http = response as? HTTPURLResponse else { throw URLError(.badServerResponse) }

        switch http.statusCode {
        case 304:
            guard let cached = entries[url] else { throw URLError(.resourceUnavailable) }
            return cached.data                                   // unchanged: reuse cached body
        case 200..<300:
            if let etag = http.value(forHTTPHeaderField: "ETag") {
                entries[url] = Entry(etag: etag, data: data)
            }
            return data
        default:
            throw URLError(.badServerResponse)
        }
    }
}
```
- In production, persist ETags and bodies (database or disk) so validation survives app restarts.

### Q34. What cache invalidation strategies exist? `[Senior]`

### Q36. How do you implement an LRU cache with a size limit? `[Senior]`
```swift
/// Least-recently-used cache with a total cost limit (e.g. bytes)
final class LRUCache<Key: Hashable, Value> {
    private final class Node {
        let key: Key
        var value: Value
        let cost: Int
        var previous: Node?
        var next: Node?
        init(key: Key, value: Value, cost: Int) { self.key = key; self.value = value; self.cost = cost }
    }

    private var nodes: [Key: Node] = [:]
    private var head: Node?           // most recently used
    private var tail: Node?           // least recently used
    private var totalCost = 0
    private let costLimit: Int

    init(costLimit: Int) { self.costLimit = costLimit }

    func value(for key: Key) -> Value? {
        guard let node = nodes[key] else { return nil }
        moveToFront(node)
        return node.value
    }

    func insert(_ value: Value, for key: Key, cost: Int) {
        if let existing = nodes[key] { remove(existing) }
        let node = Node(key: key, value: value, cost: cost)
        nodes[key] = node
        addToFront(node)
        totalCost += cost
        while totalCost > costLimit, let lru = tail { remove(lru) }    // evict least recently used
    }

    private func addToFront(_ node: Node) {
        node.next = head
        node.previous = nil
        head?.previous = node
        head = node
        if tail == nil { tail = node }
    }

    private func moveToFront(_ node: Node) {
        guard head !== node else { return }
        detach(node)
        addToFront(node)
    }

    private func remove(_ node: Node) {
        detach(node)
        nodes[node.key] = nil
        totalCost -= node.cost
    }

    private func detach(_ node: Node) {
        node.previous?.next = node.next
        node.next?.previous = node.previous
        if head === node { head = node.next }
        if tail === node { tail = node.previous }
        node.previous = nil
        node.next = nil
    }
}
```
- O(1) get and insert using a dictionary plus a doubly linked list. In an app, wrap it in an actor or a `Mutex` for thread safety, or use `NSCache` (thread-safe, evicts under memory pressure, but with non-deterministic eviction).

### Q39. What's the difference between online-first, offline-capable, and offline-first? `[Senior]`
| Approach | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Online-first** | Requires network; maybe a small cache | Banking transfers, live auctions |
| **Offline-capable** | Reads cached data offline; writes need network | News app, product catalog |

### Q40. What does a sync architecture look like? `[Lead/Expert]`
```
          ┌──────────────── App ─────────────────┐
 UI ◀────▶│ Local DB (source of truth for UI)     │
          │   ▲                    │              │
          │   │ apply changes      │ local edits  │
          │   │                    ▼              │
          │ Sync Engine ◀──── Outbox (pending ops)│
          └───┬──────────────────────┬────────────┘
              │ pull: GET /changes?since=token
              │ push: POST /operations (idempotent, batched)
              ▼
          Backend (authoritative store, change feed)
```
**Key points:**
- **UI reads only from the local database** and observes changes.
- **Local writes** update the database immediately (optimistic) and append an operation to the **outbox**.
- **Push**: the sync engine sends outbox operations (in order, with idempotency keys), removing them when acknowledged.
- **Pull**: fetches remote changes since the last token and applies them, resolving conflicts.
- **Triggers**: app launch/foreground, connectivity restored, silent push, periodic background refresh, after local writes (debounced).

```swift
                return                                         // retry later (with backoff)
            }
        }
```

### Q42. What conflict resolution strategies exist? `[Lead/Expert]`
| Strategy | How it works | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Last-write-wins (LWW)** | Latest timestamp/version wins | Simple | Silently loses edits; clock skew issues |
| **Server wins / client wins** | One side always wins | Simple, predictable | Loses the other side's changes |
| **Field-level merge** | Merge non-overlapping field changes; conflict only if the same field changed | Preserves most edits | More complex |
| **Manual resolution** | Show both versions to the user | No silent data loss | Interrupts the user |
| **Operational Transform / CRDTs** | Mathematically mergeable data structures | Real-time collaboration without conflicts | Complex; extra metadata |

```swift
/// Three-way, field-level merge using the common ancestor (base) version
struct Note: Equatable, Sendable {
    var title: String
    var body: String
    var isPinned: Bool
}

func merge(base: Note, local: Note, remote: Note) -> (merged: Note, hasConflict: Bool) {
    var merged = remote
    var hasConflict = false

    func resolve<T: Equatable>(_ keyPath: WritableKeyPath<Note, T>) {
        let localChanged = local[keyPath: keyPath] != base[keyPath: keyPath]
        let remoteChanged = remote[keyPath: keyPath] != base[keyPath: keyPath]
        switch (localChanged, remoteChanged) {
        case (true, false):
            merged[keyPath: keyPath] = local[keyPath: keyPath]      // only we changed it
        case (true, true) where local[keyPath: keyPath] != remote[keyPath: keyPath]:
            hasConflict = true                                       // both changed differently
        default:
            break                                                    // keep remote
        }
    }

    resolve(\.title)
    resolve(\.body)
    resolve(\.isPinned)
    return (merged, hasConflict)
}
```
- For conflicting fields, apply a policy (e.g. remote wins but save the local text as a "conflicted copy," as many notes apps do).

### Q43. How do you implement optimistic updates with rollback? `[Senior]`
```swift
@MainActor

final class PostViewModel {
    private(set) var isLiked: Bool
    private(set) var likeCount: Int
    private let postID: String
    private let api: PostsAPI
```

### Q44. Should IDs be generated on the client or the server? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **Client-generated UUIDs** (or ULIDs/UUIDv7 for time-sortable IDs) let the client create entities offline, reference them immediately (e.g. attach a comment to a just-created post), and use the ID as an **idempotency key**.
- Server-generated IDs require temporary local IDs and remapping after sync, which is error-prone.
- Common compromise: client UUID as the stable identity, plus a server-assigned **sequence number** for ordering (chat).

### Q45. How do you handle ordering and clock skew? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Key points:**
- Device clocks are unreliable (wrong time zones, manual changes, drift). Don't rely on client timestamps for **ordering** or conflict resolution across devices.
- Options:
  - **Server-assigned timestamps or sequence numbers** (per conversation/collection) define canonical order.
  - **Version numbers** per record for optimistic concurrency (`baseVersion` in updates; the server rejects stale versions).
  - **Hybrid logical clocks / vector clocks** for advanced multi-writer sync.
- Client timestamps are still useful for **display** ("sent 2 min ago") and local ordering of pending items.

### Q46. How do you handle local database migrations across app versions? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- Version the schema from the first release (SwiftData `VersionedSchema`, Core Data model versions, GRDB migrations).
- Migrations must handle **skipped versions** (users jumping from v1 to v5).
- For caches, it's often acceptable to **drop and rebuild** from the server instead of migrating; for user-created offline data, migrations must be lossless.
- Test migrations with real stores from previous versions; monitor launch crashes after releases.

## 7. Real-Time Communication

### Q47. Design a WebSocket client with automatic reconnection. `[Lead/Expert]`
```swift
enum ServerEvent: Decodable, Sendable {
    case message(MessageDTO)
    case typing(conversationID: String, userID: String)
    case receipt(messageID: UUID, status: String)
    // Decoding omitted for brevity
}

actor RealtimeConnection {
    let events: AsyncStream<ServerEvent>
    private let continuation: AsyncStream<ServerEvent>.Continuation
    private let url: URL
    private let session: URLSession
    private var socket: URLSessionWebSocketTask?
    private var receiveTask: Task<Void, Never>?
    private var reconnectAttempt = 0
    private var shouldBeConnected = false

    init(url: URL, session: URLSession = .shared) {
        self.url = url
        self.session = session
        (events, continuation) = AsyncStream.makeStream(of: ServerEvent.self,
                                                        bufferingPolicy: .bufferingNewest(1_000))
    }

    func connect() {
        shouldBeConnected = true
        let socket = session.webSocketTask(with: url)
        self.socket = socket
        socket.resume()
        receiveTask = Task { await receiveLoop(on: socket) }
    }

    func disconnect() {                       // e.g. when the app moves to the background
        shouldBeConnected = false
        receiveTask?.cancel()
        socket?.cancel(with: .goingAway, reason: nil)
        socket = nil
    }

    func send(_ data: Data) async throws {
        guard let socket else { throw URLError(.notConnectedToInternet) }
        try await socket.send(.data(data))
    }

    private func receiveLoop(on socket: URLSessionWebSocketTask) async {
        do {
            while !Task.isCancelled {
                let message = try await socket.receive()
                reconnectAttempt = 0                               // healthy connection
                if case .data(let data) = message,
                   let event = try? JSONDecoder().decode(ServerEvent.self, from: data) {
                    continuation.yield(event)
                }
            }
        } catch {
            await reconnectIfNeeded()
        }
    }

    private func reconnectIfNeeded() async {
        guard shouldBeConnected else { return }
        reconnectAttempt += 1
        let maxDelay = min(30, pow(2, Double(reconnectAttempt)))
        try? await Task.sleep(for: .seconds(Double.random(in: 0...maxDelay)))   // backoff + jitter
        guard shouldBeConnected, !Task.isCancelled else { return }
        connect()
        // After reconnecting, the sync layer fetches missed events using the last sequence number (Q51)
    }
}
```
**Talking points:** heartbeats (`sendPing`) to detect dead connections; reconnect on network changes (`NWPathMonitor`); disconnect in the background (rely on push); authenticate the socket (token in the handshake, refresh on expiry).

### Q48. How do you guarantee message delivery? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Key points:**
- Networks can drop messages in either direction, so aim for **at-least-once delivery + deduplication** (effectively exactly-once from the user's perspective).
- **Client → server**: messages are stored locally as `pending` with a client-generated UUID; the client retries until the server **acknowledges** that UUID; the server deduplicates by UUID.
- **Server → client**: the client acknowledges received messages (or the server infers it from sync cursors); after reconnect, the client fetches anything after its last known sequence number.
- **Status lifecycle**: `pending → sent (server ack) → delivered (recipient device ack) → read (recipient opened)`.

### Q49. How do you order messages correctly? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Key points:**
- The **server assigns a monotonically increasing sequence number per conversation** when it accepts a message; clients sort by it. This gives every participant the same order regardless of device clocks.
- Pending local messages (no sequence yet) appear at the bottom in local creation order; when acknowledged, they take their server position.
- Detect **gaps** (received seq 105 after 102): fetch 103–104 before displaying, or display with a "loading" gap marker.

```swift
struct Message: Identifiable, Codable, Sendable {
    enum Status: String, Codable, Sendable { case pending, sent, delivered, read, failed }

    let id: UUID                    // client-generated; deduplication + idempotency
    let conversationID: String
    let senderID: String
    var sequence: Int64?            // server-assigned; canonical order
    let body: String
    let createdAt: Date             // client clock: display only
    var status: Status
}

extension Array where Element == Message {
    /// Server-ordered messages first, then pending ones in local order
    func displayOrdered() -> [Message] {
        sorted { lhs, rhs in
            switch (lhs.sequence, rhs.sequence) {
            case let (l?, r?): l < r
            case (_?, nil): true
            case (nil, _?): false
            case (nil, nil): lhs.createdAt < rhs.createdAt
            }
        }
    }
}
```

### Q50. How do you implement presence and typing indicators? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- These are **ephemeral**: never persisted, never queued offline, and losing some is acceptable.
- Typing: send "typing started" at most once every few seconds while typing (throttle), and let it expire on the receiver after ~5 seconds without updates (no explicit "stopped" message required).
- Presence: derived from WebSocket connection state on the server plus heartbeats; "last seen" timestamps for offline users; respect privacy settings.
- Scale consideration: in large groups, limit or aggregate presence updates ("3 people are typing").

### Q51. How does the client recover events missed during a disconnection? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Key points:**
- Store the **last received sequence number (or sync token)** per conversation or globally.
- On reconnect, call `GET /sync?since=<token>` (or per-conversation `?after_seq=`) before (or while) resuming the live stream, then deduplicate by message ID.
- If the gap is huge (days offline), fetch recent messages first and lazily backfill older history when the user scrolls.
- The WebSocket delivers **new** events quickly; the sync endpoint guarantees **completeness**.

### Q52. How do real-time features work when the app is in the background? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- iOS suspends apps shortly after backgrounding, so WebSockets disconnect. Don't try to keep them alive.
- Use **APNs**: alert notifications for user-visible events (new message), optionally with a Notification Service Extension to decrypt or enrich content; silent pushes to prompt a sync (best effort only).
- On returning to the foreground: reconnect the socket and run a delta sync.
- Special cases: VoIP calls use PushKit + CallKit; live order/ride tracking uses **Live Activities** updated via push.

### Q55. What are silent pushes, and what are their limitations? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**

- Use them as a **hint** to sync ("new data available"), backed by sync on launch/foreground.

### Q56. What is a Notification Service Extension, and when would you use it? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- An app extension that can **modify a notification's content before it's displayed** (when the payload contains `"mutable-content": 1`). It gets a short time window (~30 seconds).
- Use cases: downloading and attaching images/video thumbnails, **decrypting end-to-end encrypted** messages, localizing or personalizing content, and updating badge counts from shared storage.

```swift
import UserNotifications

final class NotificationService: UNNotificationServiceExtension {
    private var contentHandler: ((UNNotificationContent) -> Void)?
    private var bestAttempt: UNMutableNotificationContent?

    override func didReceive(_ request: UNNotificationRequest,
                             withContentHandler contentHandler: @escaping (UNNotificationContent) -> Void) {
        self.contentHandler = contentHandler
        bestAttempt = request.content.mutableCopy() as? UNMutableNotificationContent

        guard let content = bestAttempt,
              let urlString = content.userInfo["image_url"] as? String,
              let url = URL(string: urlString) else {
            contentHandler(request.content)
            return
        }

        Task {
            if let (fileURL, _) = try? await URLSession.shared.download(from: url) {
                let destination = FileManager.default.temporaryDirectory
                    .appending(path: UUID().uuidString + ".jpg")
                try? FileManager.default.moveItem(at: fileURL, to: destination)
                if let attachment = try? UNNotificationAttachment(identifier: "image", url: destination) {
                    content.attachments = [attachment]
                }
            }
            contentHandler(content)
        }
    }

    override func serviceExtensionTimeWillExpire() {
        // Deliver whatever we have before the system kills the extension
        if let contentHandler, let bestAttempt { contentHandler(bestAttempt) }
    }
}
```

### Q57. What delivery guarantees does APNs provide? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **Best effort, no guaranteed delivery**. If the device is offline, APNs stores **only the most recent** notification per app (per collapse ID) for a limited time, then delivers when the device reconnects.
- Useful headers: `apns-expiration` (when to give up), `apns-collapse-id` (replace older notifications, e.g. "score update"), `apns-priority` (10 = immediate, 5 = power-efficient), `apns-push-type`.
- Payload limit: **4 KB** for regular notifications, so send identifiers and fetch details in the app or the service extension.
- Therefore the app must **never rely on push as the only delivery channel** for important data. Sync on launch.

### Q58. How do you design a good notification strategy? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Key points:**
- **Permission priming**: ask at a meaningful moment (after the user follows someone or places an order), explaining the value; consider **provisional authorization** (quiet delivery first).
- **Categories and actions**: reply, mark as read, accept/decline directly from the notification.
- **Relevance and interruption levels** (passive, active, time-sensitive, critical with entitlement); grouping by thread.
- **User control**: granular in-app settings synced with the backend; quiet hours; frequency caps on the server.
- **Live Activities** for ongoing events (delivery, rides, sports), updated via ActivityKit push.
- **Measure**: opt-in rate, open rate, opt-out/disable rate; avoid notification fatigue.

### Q59. What background execution options does iOS offer? `[Senior]`
| Mechanism | Use case | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| `beginBackgroundTask` | Finish a short task when the app is backgrounded (save, upload a small request) | Limited time (often around 30 seconds); must end it |
| `BGAppRefreshTask` | Periodic content refresh (feed, inbox) | System decides when (usage patterns, battery); ~30 s of runtime |

| Background `URLSession` | Uploads/downloads that continue while suspended or terminated | Performed by the system; discretionary scheduling possible |
| Silent push | Server-triggered sync | Best effort, throttled |
| Background modes | Audio, location, VoIP (PushKit), Bluetooth, external accessory | Must match real functionality (App Review) |

### Q60. How do you schedule background refresh with `BGTaskScheduler`? `[Senior]`
```swift
import BackgroundTasks

enum BackgroundRefresh {
    static let identifier = "com.example.app.refresh"   // also listed in Info.plist (BGTaskSchedulerPermittedIdentifiers)

    /// Call before the app finishes launching (e.g. in application(_:didFinishLaunchingWithOptions:))
    static func register(syncEngine: SyncEngine) {
        BGTaskScheduler.shared.register(forTaskWithIdentifier: identifier, using: nil) { task in
            guard let task = task as? BGAppRefreshTask else { return }
            handle(task, syncEngine: syncEngine)
        }
    }

    static func schedule() {
        let request = BGAppRefreshTaskRequest(identifier: identifier)
        request.earliestBeginDate = Date(timeIntervalSinceNow: 30 * 60)   // a hint, not a guarantee
        try? BGTaskScheduler.shared.submit(request)
    }

    private static func handle(_ task: BGAppRefreshTask, syncEngine: SyncEngine) {
        schedule()                                                        // keep the chain going

        let work = Task {
            await syncEngine.pushPendingOperations()
            let success = (try? await syncEngine.pullChanges()) != nil
            task.setTaskCompleted(success: success)
        }

        task.expirationHandler = {
            work.cancel()                       // the system is out of patience: stop cooperatively
        }
    }
}
```
- SwiftUI apps can use `.backgroundTask(.appRefresh(identifier)) { await ... }` on the scene instead.

### Q61. When would you use a background `URLSession`? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- For **large uploads/downloads** that should continue even if the app is suspended or terminated by the system (photo/video uploads, offline media downloads).
- Requirements: a `URLSessionConfiguration.background(withIdentifier:)` session with a **delegate** (no async/await convenience APIs); upload **from a file** (not in-memory data); handle `application(_:handleEventsForBackgroundURLSession:completionHandler:)` to process results after relaunch.
- Options: `isDiscretionary = true` lets the system wait for Wi-Fi and power (good for non-urgent transfers); `sessionSendsLaunchEvents = true` relaunches the app in the background when transfers finish.

```swift
let configuration = URLSessionConfiguration.background(withIdentifier: "com.example.app.uploads")
configuration.isDiscretionary = false            // user-initiated: start as soon as possible
configuration.sessionSendsLaunchEvents = true
configuration.allowsCellularAccess = true
let uploadSession = URLSession(configuration: configuration, delegate: uploadDelegate, delegateQueue: nil)

var request = URLRequest(url: presignedUploadURL)
request.httpMethod = "PUT"
let task = uploadSession.uploadTask(with: request, fromFile: compressedVideoFileURL)
task.taskDescription = uploadID.uuidString        // correlate results after relaunch
task.resume()
```

### Q62. How do you design features knowing background execution isn't guaranteed? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Key points:**
- Treat background work as an **optimization**, never a correctness requirement. Every important sync must also happen on launch/foreground.
- Persist intent (outbox, pending uploads) so work resumes whenever the app next runs.
- Make operations **idempotent and resumable** (chunked uploads, sync tokens).
- Remember: if the user **force-quits** the app, most background mechanisms (silent push, background refresh) stop until the user opens it again.
- Communicate state in the UI ("Waiting to upload, 3 items").

### Q64. How do you choose image sizes and formats? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- Request **renditions sized for the device**: the client sends the target size and scale, and the CDN resizes on the fly (e.g. `https://cdn.example.com/img/abc.jpg?w=750&q=75&fm=webp`).
- Modern formats cut size dramatically: **HEIC**, **WebP** (iOS 14+), **AVIF** (iOS 16+ decoding). Fall back to JPEG where needed.
- Lower quality for Low Data Mode and thumbnails; progressive loading (thumbnail first, full image on demand).
- Strip metadata (EXIF location) on upload for privacy.

### Q65. How does video streaming work on iOS? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **HLS (HTTP Live Streaming)**: video is split into short segments at multiple bitrates, listed in playlists (`.m3u8`). `AVPlayer` performs **adaptive bitrate (ABR)** switching based on bandwidth.
- Served from a **CDN**, close to users, cacheable.
- **DRM** with FairPlay Streaming for premium content.
- Client concerns: startup time (preload the first segments, lower initial bitrate), rebuffering rate, battery, data usage (cap resolution on cellular/Low Data Mode via `preferredPeakBitRate`), and Picture in Picture / background audio.

### Q66. How do you handle autoplaying video in a scrolling feed? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Key points:**
- Play **only the most visible** video (visibility threshold, e.g. > 60% on screen); pause others.
- **Reuse a small pool of `AVPlayer` instances** (creating players is expensive) and attach them to visible cells.
- **Preload** the next video's first segments (or a short MP4 preview) when it's about to appear; cancel when it scrolls away.
- Muted by default; show a poster frame until the first frame renders; respect Reduce Motion, Low Data Mode, and an "autoplay on Wi-Fi only" setting.
- Measure startup latency, rebuffering, and memory.

### Q67. How do you upload large files reliably? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Key points:**
- **Presigned URLs**: the app asks the backend for an upload URL, uploads directly to object storage (S3/GCS) or a CDN edge, then confirms with the backend. This keeps large files off API servers.
- **Chunked / resumable uploads** (multipart uploads, or a protocol like tus): split into chunks (e.g. 5–10 MB), upload chunks independently with retries, and resume from the last confirmed chunk after failures or app restarts.
- **Background `URLSession`** so uploads continue when the app is suspended.
- **Idempotency**: a client-generated upload ID ties chunks and the final "complete" call together.

```swift
struct UploadSession: Codable, Sendable {
    let uploadID: UUID
    let fileURL: URL
    let chunkSize: Int
    let totalChunks: Int
    var completedChunks: Set<Int>
}

actor ChunkedUploader {
    private let api: UploadAPI
    private let store: UploadSessionStore         // persisted so uploads resume after relaunch

    init(api: UploadAPI, store: UploadSessionStore) {
        self.api = api
        self.store = store
    }

    func upload(_ session: UploadSession) async throws -> RemoteMediaID {
        var session = session
        let handle = try FileHandle(forReadingFrom: session.fileURL)
        defer { try? handle.close() }

        for index in 0..<session.totalChunks where !session.completedChunks.contains(index) {
            try Task.checkCancellation()
            try handle.seek(toOffset: UInt64(index * session.chunkSize))
            guard let chunk = try handle.read(upToCount: session.chunkSize) else { break }

            let presignedURL = try await api.chunkURL(uploadID: session.uploadID, index: index)
            try await withRetry(maxAttempts: 3) {
                try await api.put(chunk, to: presignedURL)
            }

            session.completedChunks.insert(index)
            try await store.save(session)            // checkpoint progress
        }

        return try await api.completeUpload(uploadID: session.uploadID)   // server assembles chunks
    }
}
```

### Q68. Should media be compressed or transcoded on the device before upload? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **Yes, usually**: resizing photos (e.g. max 2048 px), converting to HEIC/JPEG at reasonable quality, and transcoding videos (H.264/HEVC at a target bitrate with `AVAssetExportSession`) can cut upload size by 5–20×, which saves time, data, and battery.
- Trade-offs: CPU/battery cost on device, time before upload starts, and quality loss. For pro/creator apps, upload originals on Wi-Fi.
- Do it in the background with progress, and keep the compressed file on disk so retries don't redo the work.

### Q69. How do you support offline media downloads? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- For HLS video, use `AVAssetDownloadURLSession` (supports FairPlay offline licenses); for files, a background `URLSession`.
- Track downloads in a database (status, progress, size, expiry, license expiry).
- Store in `Application Support` (not `Caches`, which the system can purge) and exclude from iCloud backup.
- Offer storage management: total size, delete per item, automatic deletion after watching, Wi-Fi-only downloads.

### Q70. What image placeholder strategies improve perceived performance? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **Dominant color** placeholder (from the API) for instant, layout-stable boxes.
- **BlurHash / ThumbHash** strings in the JSON (tens of bytes) decoded to a blurry preview.
- **Low-quality image placeholder (LQIP)**: a tiny thumbnail loaded first, replaced by the full image.
- Always reserve the image's final **aspect ratio** (send width/height in the API) to avoid layout shifts.

## 11. Security & Privacy

### Q71. How should a mobile app authenticate users? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **OAuth 2.0 Authorization Code flow with PKCE** via `ASWebAuthenticationSession` for third-party/SSO logins. PKCE prevents intercepted authorization codes from being exchanged by attackers.
- **Sign in with Apple** (required in some cases when offering other social logins) and **passkeys** (`ASAuthorization` with platform public-key credentials) for phishing-resistant, passwordless login.
- Short-lived **access tokens** + longer-lived **refresh tokens**; never embed client secrets in the app.

```swift
import CryptoKit
import Foundation

struct PKCE {
    let verifier: String
    let challenge: String

    init() {
        var bytes = [UInt8](repeating: 0, count: 32)
        _ = SecRandomCopyBytes(kSecRandomDefault, bytes.count, &bytes)
        verifier = Data(bytes).base64URLEncodedString()
        challenge = Data(SHA256.hash(data: Data(verifier.utf8))).base64URLEncodedString()
    }
}

extension Data {
    func base64URLEncodedString() -> String {
        base64EncodedString()
            .replacingOccurrences(of: "+", with: "-")
            .replacingOccurrences(of: "/", with: "_")
            .replacingOccurrences(of: "=", with: "")
    }
}

// 1. Open: https://auth.example.com/authorize?response_type=code&client_id=ios-app
//          &redirect_uri=myapp://callback&code_challenge=<challenge>&code_challenge_method=S256
// 2. Receive myapp://callback?code=XYZ via ASWebAuthenticationSession
// 3. POST /token with code + code_verifier=<verifier> → access_token, refresh_token
```

### Q72. Where should tokens be stored? `[Fundamental]`
```swift
import Security

enum KeychainError: Error { case unexpectedStatus(OSStatus) }

enum Keychain {
    static func save(_ data: Data, account: String, service: String = "com.example.app.auth") throws {
        let query: [String: Any] = [
            kSecClass as String: kSecClassGenericPassword,
            kSecAttrService as String: service,
            kSecAttrAccount as String: account,
        ]
        SecItemDelete(query as CFDictionary)                     // replace any existing item

        var attributes = query
        attributes[kSecValueData as String] = data
        attributes[kSecAttrAccessible as String] = kSecAttrAccessibleAfterFirstUnlockThisDeviceOnly

        let status = SecItemAdd(attributes as CFDictionary, nil)
        guard status == errSecSuccess else { throw KeychainError.unexpectedStatus(status) }
    }

    static func load(account: String, service: String = "com.example.app.auth") throws -> Data? {
        let query: [String: Any] = [
            kSecClass as String: kSecClassGenericPassword,
            kSecAttrService as String: service,
            kSecAttrAccount as String: account,
            kSecReturnData as String: true,
            kSecMatchLimit as String: kSecMatchLimitOne,
        ]
        var result: AnyObject?
        let status = SecItemCopyMatching(query as CFDictionary, &result)
        if status == errSecItemNotFound { return nil }
        guard status == errSecSuccess else { throw KeychainError.unexpectedStatus(status) }
        return result as? Data
    }
}
```
**Talking points:**
- **Keychain**, never `UserDefaults` or plain files.
- Accessibility class: `AfterFirstUnlockThisDeviceOnly` lets background tasks read tokens after the first unlock, and `ThisDeviceOnly` keeps tokens out of backups/restores to other devices.
- Share with extensions via a **Keychain access group**; consider Secure Enclave keys for high-security apps.
- Note: Keychain items can **survive app deletion**, so clear stale tokens on first launch after reinstall if needed.

### Q73. How should token refresh work at the system level? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- The networking layer attaches access tokens via an interceptor and proactively refreshes shortly before expiry.
- On 401, **one shared refresh** runs (concurrent requests wait for it), then failed requests are retried once (implementation: Async/Await file, Q100).
- If refresh fails (revoked/expired), clear credentials and route to login, preserving the user's place where possible.
- Backend side: rotate refresh tokens, detect reuse (possible theft), and allow remote revocation (e.g. "log out of all devices").

### Q74. What is certificate pinning, and what are its risks? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Key points:**
- Pinning makes the app trust only specific certificates or public keys for your domains, defending against man-in-the-middle attacks with rogue or compromised CAs (and making traffic inspection harder).
- **Declarative option (iOS 14+)**: `NSPinnedDomains` in Info.plist (App Transport Security) with SPKI SHA-256 hashes of CA or leaf keys. This is simpler and less error-prone than custom code.

```xml
<key>NSAppTransportSecurity</key>
<dict>
    <key>NSPinnedDomains</key>
    <dict>
        <key>api.example.com</key>
        <dict>
            <key>NSIncludesSubdomains</key>
            <true/>
            <key>NSPinnedCAIdentities</key>
            <array>
                <dict>
                    <key>SPKI-SHA256-BASE64</key>
                    <string>r/mIkG3eEpVdm+u/ko/cwxzOMo1bk4TyHIlByibiA5E=</string>
                </dict>
                <dict>
                    <key>SPKI-SHA256-BASE64</key>
                    <string>BACKUP_PIN_FOR_ROTATION_BASE64=</string>
                </dict>
            </array>
        </dict>
    </dict>
</dict>
```
- **Risks**: if certificates rotate and pins don't match, **every installed app version loses connectivity** until users update (and old versions may never update). Mitigate with backup pins, pinning to intermediate/CA keys rather than leaf certificates, monitoring, and coordinated rotation plans.
- Pinning is common for finance/health apps; many consumer apps skip it and rely on standard TLS + ATS.

### Q75. How do you protect data at rest on the device? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **Data Protection** classes encrypt files with keys tied to the passcode:
  - `.complete`: accessible only while unlocked (most sensitive data, but unavailable to background tasks while locked).
  - `.completeUnlessOpen`: files already open stay accessible after locking (e.g. ongoing downloads).
  - `.completeUntilFirstUserAuthentication` (default): accessible after the first unlock since boot.
- Encrypt especially sensitive databases (e.g. SQLCipher) or fields (CryptoKit with Keychain-stored keys).
- Minimize stored PII; delete data on logout; exclude sensitive files from backups; avoid sensitive data in logs and screenshots (hide content in the app switcher snapshot).

### Q76. Can you store API keys or secrets in the app? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **Assume anything in the app binary can be extracted** (strings, obfuscation, encrypted constants can all be reverse engineered).
- Don't ship secrets that grant privileged access. Use the backend as a proxy for third-party APIs needing secret keys; issue **short-lived, scoped tokens** to the app.
- Publishable keys (e.g. public SDK keys meant for clients) are fine, but restrict them server-side (bundle ID, rate limits).
- Use **App Attest** (DeviceCheck framework) so your backend can verify requests come from a genuine instance of your app, reducing abuse from scripts and modified clients.

### Q77. Should you implement jailbreak detection? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Key points:**
- Jailbreak detection and runtime protection can raise the bar for attackers but are **always bypassable** on a compromised device.
- Use as one layer in **defense in depth** for high-risk apps (banking, DRM): combine with server-side risk scoring, App Attest, transaction limits, and anomaly detection.
- Avoid relying on client checks for security decisions; the **server** must enforce authorization and validation.

### Q78. How does end-to-end encryption affect a chat app design? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Key points:**
- Messages are encrypted on the sender's device and decrypted only on recipients' devices; the server routes ciphertext it can't read.
- Typically based on the **Signal Protocol** (identity keys, prekeys, Double Ratchet for forward secrecy), with per-device keys for multi-device support.
- Design impacts:
  - Server-side search, moderation, and previews aren't possible on plaintext. Push notifications carry ciphertext or just IDs, and a **Notification Service Extension** decrypts and displays content.
  - Key storage in the Keychain/Secure Enclave; key verification UI (safety numbers); encrypted backups need separate key management.
  - Group chats require sender keys or pairwise encryption; adding devices requires key distribution.

### Q79. What privacy requirements shape mobile system design? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **Privacy manifests** (`PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`): declare data collected, tracking domains, and reasons for using certain "required reason" APIs; third-party SDKs must include their own.
- **App Tracking Transparency**: permission required before tracking users across apps/websites (IDFA).
- **App Store privacy labels** must match actual data collection.
- **Data minimization**: collect only what's needed; prefer on-device processing (Vision, Core ML, Foundation Models where available).
- **User rights**: account deletion inside the app (an App Store requirement), data export, consent management, regional regulations (GDPR, CCPA).
- **Permissions**: request in context, degrade gracefully when denied (e.g. approximate location, limited photo library access).

## 12. Performance & Resource Constraints

### Q80. How do you design for fast app launch? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- Keep pre-main work low: fewer dynamic frameworks (or mergeable libraries), no heavy static initializers.
- In `didFinishLaunching`: only essential setup (crash reporting, dependency container). Defer analytics, remote config fetches, prefetching, and non-critical SDKs until after the first frame.
- Render the first screen from **local cached data**; fetch fresh data asynchronously.
- Avoid synchronous disk or network I/O on the main thread at launch.
- Measure cold/warm launch with Instruments (App Launch) and in production (Xcode Organizer, MetricKit); set budgets and alert on regressions.

### Q81. How do you design within memory limits? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- iOS terminates apps that exceed memory limits (**jetsam**), often without a crash report, which looks like a "silent crash" to users.
- Strategies: downsampled images; `NSCache` (auto-evicts) instead of unbounded dictionaries; windowed lists for huge datasets; stream large files instead of loading them fully; release caches on memory warnings and when backgrounded.
- Monitor: MetricKit exit reasons (memory limit terminations), Instruments Allocations/Leaks, memory graph.

### Q82. How do you design for battery efficiency? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **Network**: batch requests (analytics), avoid frequent polling, prefer push, use discretionary background transfers, and coalesce work when the radio is already active.
- **Location**: the lowest accuracy that meets requirements; significant-change or visit monitoring instead of continuous GPS; stop updates when not needed.
- **CPU/GPU**: avoid unnecessary animations and timers; pause work when off-screen or backgrounded; offload heavy work efficiently (no busy loops).
- **Background**: respect system scheduling; don't abuse background modes.
- Measure with Xcode's Energy gauge, Instruments (Energy Log/Power Profiler), and MetricKit energy metrics.

### Q83. How do you minimize network usage? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- Right-sized media and modern formats (the biggest win); compression for JSON; delta sync instead of full reloads; HTTP caching and conditional requests.
- **Low Data Mode**: `allowsConstrainedNetworkAccess = false` for prefetching and high-resolution media; lower video bitrates.
- Prefetch on Wi-Fi only, and only what users are likely to view.
- Batch analytics uploads; avoid sending duplicate data.

### Q84. How do you keep app size under control? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- Asset catalogs with app thinning (device-specific assets), vector or SF Symbols where possible, compressed images.
- Remove unused code and resources; audit third-party SDK sizes; avoid duplicated dependencies across extensions.
- **On-demand resources** or **Background Assets** (downloading large content after install) for game levels, ML models, or rarely used features.
- Track app size per release in CI (App Store Connect reports download/install sizes); large downloads on cellular can prompt users and reduce installs.

### Q85. How do you support low-end devices and older iOS versions? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- Choose a minimum iOS version based on user analytics and API needs.
- Adapt quality to device capabilities: fewer simultaneous video players, lower image resolutions, reduced effects (blur, heavy shadows), simpler animations on older devices or in Low Power Mode (`ProcessInfo.isLowPowerModeEnabled`).
- Test on the oldest supported devices; measure hitch rates and memory on them specifically.
- Use `#available` with graceful fallbacks for newer APIs.

### Q86. How do you keep scrolling smooth with large, complex lists? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- Lazy containers with cell reuse (`UICollectionView`, `List`), stable identifiers, and diffable data sources.
- Precompute layout and display strings off the main thread; cache heights where needed.
- Image pipeline with downsampling and cancellation; avoid offscreen rendering (shadows without paths, masks).
- Paginate and window very large datasets from the local database.

### Q88. How do you set up crash reporting effectively? `[Senior]`

### Q90. How do you trace requests from the app to the backend? `[Lead/Expert]`
```swift
struct TracingInterceptor: RequestInterceptor {
    func intercept(_ request: URLRequest) async throws -> URLRequest {
        var request = request
        let traceID = UUID().uuidString.replacingOccurrences(of: "-", with: "").lowercased()
        let spanID = String(traceID.prefix(16))
        // W3C Trace Context header, understood by most backend tracing systems
        request.setValue("00-\(traceID)-\(spanID)-01", forHTTPHeaderField: "traceparent")
        request.setValue(UUID().uuidString, forHTTPHeaderField: "X-Request-ID")
        return request
    }
}
```

## 14. Release, Experimentation & Server-Driven UI

### Q94. How do mobile release constraints differ from web? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **No instant deploys**: App Review takes time; users update slowly; old versions stay in use for months.
- **No rollback**: you can't revert an installed binary, only ship a fix (or disable features remotely).
- Therefore mobile teams rely on **feature flags, phased releases, kill switches, backward-compatible APIs, and thorough pre-release testing** (beta via TestFlight, internal dogfooding).

### Q95. How do phased rollouts and kill switches work together? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- **App Store phased release**: automatic updates roll out over 7 days (1%, 2%, 5%, 10%, 20%, 50%, 100%) and can be paused if metrics look bad. Users can still update manually.
- **Feature flags** decouple deployment from release: ship code dark, enable gradually per cohort/percentage, and roll back instantly by turning the flag off.
- **Kill switches**: flags that disable a risky feature (or route to a fallback flow) during incidents.
- Monitor crash and business metrics at each stage before expanding.

### Q96. How do you design a force-update / minimum-version mechanism? `[Senior]`
```swift
struct VersionPolicy: Decodable, Sendable {
    let minimumSupported: String     // below this: block with "Update required"
    let recommended: String          // below this: dismissible "Update available" prompt
    let storeURL: URL
}

enum UpdateRequirement: Equatable {
    case none, recommended(URL), required(URL)
}

func evaluate(policy: VersionPolicy, currentVersion: String) -> UpdateRequirement {
    func isOlder(_ lhs: String, than rhs: String) -> Bool {
        lhs.compare(rhs, options: .numeric) == .orderedAscending      // "2.10.0" > "2.9.3"
    }
    if isOlder(currentVersion, than: policy.minimumSupported) { return .required(policy.storeURL) }
    if isOlder(currentVersion, than: policy.recommended) { return .recommended(policy.storeURL) }
    return .none
}
```

**Talking points:**
- Fetched at launch from a lightweight endpoint (or remote config), cached, and failing **open** (if the endpoint is down, don't block users).
- The API can also return a specific error (e.g. `426 Upgrade Required` or a typed error code) for endpoints that no longer support old versions.
- Use sparingly: forced updates frustrate users. Prefer backward compatibility.

### Q97. How does A/B testing work on mobile? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Key points:**
- **Assignment**: deterministic bucketing on the server or client (hash of user ID + experiment ID → variant), so users consistently see the same variant across sessions and devices.
- **Delivery**: variants fetched with remote config at launch and cached; avoid flicker by applying assignments before showing affected screens (or keep the previous session's assignment until the next launch).
- **Exposure logging**: log an exposure event when the user actually **sees** the variant (not when it's assigned), otherwise results are diluted.
- **Analysis**: predefined metrics, sufficient sample size, guardrail metrics (crashes, latency).
- **Hygiene**: owners and end dates for experiments; remove losing code paths.

### Q101. What principles guide designing a third-party iOS SDK? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Key points:**
- **Never crash or slow down the host app**: catch errors internally, do work off the main thread, bounded memory and disk use, no blocking calls at launch.

```swift
final class ImpressionTracker {

}
```

```swift
actor ChatService {
    private let store: MessageStore
    private let outbox: MessageOutbox
    private let connection: RealtimeConnection

    init(store: MessageStore, outbox: MessageOutbox, connection: RealtimeConnection) {
        self.store = store
        self.outbox = outbox
        self.connection = connection
    }

    /// 1. Send: persist as pending first, so it survives crashes and shows instantly
    func send(text: String, conversationID: String, senderID: String) async {
        let message = Message(id: UUID(), conversationID: conversationID, senderID: senderID,
                              sequence: nil, body: text, createdAt: .now, status: .pending)
        try? await store.insert(message)
        await outbox.enqueue(message.id)
        await flushOutbox()
    }

    /// 2. Ack from server: assign canonical order and mark as sent
    func handleAck(clientID: UUID, sequence: Int64) async {
        try? await store.markSent(clientID, sequence: sequence)
        await outbox.remove(clientID)
    }

    /// 3. Incoming message: dedupe, store, acknowledge delivery
    func handleIncoming(_ dto: MessageDTO) async {
        if (try? await store.contains(dto.clientID)) == true { return }     // at-least-once → dedupe
        try? await store.insert(Message(dto: dto))
        try? await connection.send(ReceiptCommand.delivered(upTo: dto.sequence,
                                                            conversationID: dto.conversationID).encoded())
    }

    /// 4. Retry pending messages (on reconnect, foreground, or timer)
    func flushOutbox() async {
        for id in await outbox.pendingIDs() {
            guard let message = try? await store.message(id: id) else { continue }
            try? await connection.send(SendMessageCommand(message).encoded())
            // Remains in the outbox until handleAck; retried with backoff if no ack arrives
        }
    }
}
```
- **Reconnect**: WebSocket reconnects → `GET /sync?since=token` fills gaps → dedupe → resume live events (Q51).

**6. Deep dives**
- **Ordering**: server sequence per conversation; pending messages at the bottom (Q49).
- **Delivery guarantees**: at-least-once with idempotent client IDs (Q48).
- **Media messages**: compress → upload to presigned URL → send a message referencing `media_id`; show the local image immediately with upload progress.
- **Read receipts**: send "read up to sequence N" (one message per conversation, throttled) rather than per message.
- **Groups**: server fan-out; receipts aggregated ("Read by 5"); typing indicators throttled.
- **Multi-device**: each device has its own sync token; read state synced so notifications clear on other devices.
- **Storage**: text kept locally; media cache bounded with "manage storage" UI.
- **E2E encryption** (extension): Signal Protocol; NSE decrypts notifications; server can't index content (Q78).

**7. Trade-offs**
- WebSocket vs HTTP long polling fallback (corporate networks may block WebSockets).
- Storing full history locally (fast, offline search) vs on-demand loading (less storage).
- Server-side search vs E2E encryption.

### Q107. Design an image loading and caching library (like Kingfisher / Nuke / SDWebImage). `[Lead/Expert]`

- Non-functional: no main-thread decoding; bounded memory and disk; thread-safe; simple API; high cache hit rate; small binary size.

**2. Architecture**
```

        │
        ▼

   ├── MemoryCache  (NSCache of decoded, downsampled images; key = URL + target size)
   ├── DiskCache    (files keyed by URL hash; LRU eviction by total bytes; expiry)
   ├── DataLoader   (URLSession; priorities; limited concurrency; HTTP cache headers)
   └── Decoder      (ImageIO downsampling off the actor, in a background task)
```

**3. API design**
```swift
struct ImageRequest: Hashable, Sendable {
    let url: URL
    let width: CGFloat          // target size in points
    let height: CGFloat
    let scale: CGFloat

    var cacheKey: String { "\(url.absoluteString)#\(Int(width))x\(Int(height))@\(scale)" }
    var maxPixelSize: CGFloat { max(width, height) * scale }
}

enum ImageError: Error { case decodingFailed, badResponse }
```

**4. Core pipeline**
```swift
import ImageIO
import UIKit

actor ImagePipeline {
    static let shared = ImagePipeline()

    private let memoryCache = NSCache<NSString, UIImage>()
    private let diskCache: DiskCache                       // actor: get/set Data, LRU eviction
    private let session: URLSession
    private var inFlight: [String: Task<UIImage, Error>] = [:]

    init(diskCache: DiskCache = DiskCache(name: "images", byteLimit: 200 * 1024 * 1024),
         session: URLSession = .shared) {
        self.diskCache = diskCache
        self.session = session
        memoryCache.totalCostLimit = 100 * 1024 * 1024     // ~100 MB of decoded images
    }

    func image(for request: ImageRequest) async throws -> UIImage {
        let key = request.cacheKey
        if let cached = memoryCache.object(forKey: key as NSString) { return cached }   // 1. memory
        if let existing = inFlight[key] { return try await existing.value }              // 2. join in-flight

        let task = Task.detached(priority: .userInitiated) { [diskCache, session] () throws -> UIImage in
            let data: Data
            if let cachedData = await diskCache.data(forKey: request.url.absoluteString) {   // 3. disk
                data = cachedData
            } else {
                let (downloaded, response) = try await session.data(from: request.url)       // 4. network
                guard (response as? HTTPURLResponse)?.statusCode == 200 else { throw ImageError.badResponse }
                await diskCache.store(downloaded, forKey: request.url.absoluteString)
                data = downloaded
            }
            try Task.checkCancellation()
            guard let image = Self.downsample(data, maxPixelSize: request.maxPixelSize) else {
                throw ImageError.decodingFailed
            }
            return image
        }

        inFlight[key] = task
        defer { inFlight[key] = nil }

        let image = try await task.value
        memoryCache.setObject(image, forKey: key as NSString, cost: image.decodedByteCount)
        return image
    }

    nonisolated private static func downsample(_ data: Data, maxPixelSize: CGFloat) -> UIImage? {
        let sourceOptions = [kCGImageSourceShouldCache: false] as CFDictionary
        guard let source = CGImageSourceCreateWithData(data as CFData, sourceOptions) else { return nil }
        let options = [
            kCGImageSourceCreateThumbnailFromImageAlways: true,
            kCGImageSourceShouldCacheImmediately: true,          // decode now, off the main thread
            kCGImageSourceCreateThumbnailWithTransform: true,
            kCGImageSourceThumbnailMaxPixelSize: maxPixelSize,
        ] as CFDictionary
        guard let cgImage = CGImageSourceCreateThumbnailAtIndex(source, 0, options) else { return nil }
        return UIImage(cgImage: cgImage)
    }
}

extension UIImage {
    var decodedByteCount: Int { Int(size.width * scale * size.height * scale * 4) }
}

// SwiftUI integration: cancellation comes for free with .task(id:)
struct RemoteImage: View {
    let url: URL?
    @State private var image: UIImage?
    @Environment(\.displayScale) private var displayScale

    var body: some View {
        GeometryReader { proxy in
            Group {
                if let image {
                    Image(uiImage: image).resizable().scaledToFill()
                } else {
                    Rectangle().fill(.quaternary)                   // placeholder
                }
            }
            .frame(width: proxy.size.width, height: proxy.size.height)
            .clipped()
            .task(id: url) {
                guard let url else { return }
                let request = ImageRequest(url: url, width: proxy.size.width,
                                           height: proxy.size.height, scale: displayScale)
                image = try? await ImagePipeline.shared.image(for: request)
            }
        }
    }
}
```

**5. Deep dives**
- **Cancellation with deduplication**: if two views share one in-flight task, cancelling one view shouldn't cancel the download for the other. Keep a **subscriber count** per task and cancel only when it reaches zero.
- **Disk cache**: files named by a hash of the URL; an index (or file attributes) for last access and size; LRU eviction when over the byte limit; respect HTTP cache headers or a max age; store in `Caches/` (purgeable).
- **Memory pressure**: `NSCache` evicts automatically; also clear on memory warnings and trim when backgrounded.
- **Prefetching**: low priority; cancel when rows scroll away; skip in Low Data Mode.
- **Priorities and concurrency**: visible requests high priority; limit concurrent downloads (e.g. 6) to avoid saturating the network.
- **Metrics**: memory/disk hit rates, decode time, bytes downloaded.

**6. Trade-offs**
- Caching decoded images (fast display, high memory) vs encoded data (low memory, decode cost).
- Size-specific cache keys (more entries, exact fit) vs one size per URL (fewer entries, extra resizing).
- Building in-house vs adopting Nuke/Kingfisher (maintenance cost vs control).

**5. Key flows**
- **Edit offline**: update the local record (instant UI) → enqueue an `update` op with `base_version` → sync later.
- **Push**: server applies if `base_version == current_version`; otherwise returns `conflict` with the server note.
- **Conflict**: three-way merge (base, local, remote) field by field (Q42); for conflicting body edits, keep the server version and create a "Conflicted copy" note so nothing is lost.
- **Pull**: apply remote upserts unless the note has pending local ops (then merge); apply tombstones; save the new token.
- **Deletes**: tombstones locally and on the server so other devices learn about deletions; purge tombstones after confirmed sync.

**6. Deep dives**
- **Search**: SQLite FTS5 (or Core Spotlight) on device, updated on writes; works offline.
- **Attachments**: uploaded separately; notes reference attachment IDs; placeholders while uploading; downloaded lazily on other devices.
- **First sync on a new device**: paged `changes` from token 0, newest notes first so the UI is useful quickly.
- **Token expiry**: if the server has compacted history beyond the client's token, it requests a full resync (client merges carefully with local unsynced changes).
- **Real-time collaboration** (extension): CRDT-based text (e.g. sequence CRDTs) to merge concurrent character edits automatically.
- **Encryption** (extension): end-to-end encrypted notes require client-side encryption and key management; the server can't merge plaintext.

**7. Trade-offs**
- Operation-based sync (precise, needs ordering) vs state-based (simpler, larger payloads).
- Field-level merge + conflicted copies vs last-write-wins (data safety vs simplicity).
- CRDTs (automatic merges) vs complexity and metadata overhead.

**3. Implementation**
```swift
@MainActor
@Observable
final class SearchViewModel {
    var query = "" {
        didSet { scheduleSuggestions(for: query) }
    }
    private(set) var suggestions: [Suggestion] = []

    private let api: SearchAPI
    private let clock: any Clock<Duration>
    private var cache: [String: [Suggestion]] = [:]       // bounded LRU in production
    private var suggestionTask: Task<Void, Never>?

    init(api: SearchAPI, clock: any Clock<Duration> = ContinuousClock()) {
        self.api = api
        self.clock = clock
    }

    private func scheduleSuggestions(for rawQuery: String) {
        suggestionTask?.cancel()                           // the newest query always wins
        let normalized = rawQuery.trimmingCharacters(in: .whitespaces).lowercased()

        guard normalized.count >= 2 else {
            suggestions = []
            return
        }
        if let cached = cache[normalized] {                // instant for repeated prefixes (e.g. backspace)
            suggestions = cached
            return
        }

        suggestionTask = Task {
            do {
                try await clock.sleep(for: .milliseconds(250))      // debounce
                let results = try await api.suggest(prefix: normalized, limit: 8)
                cache[normalized] = results
                suggestions = results                               // only reached if not cancelled
            } catch {
                // CancellationError: superseded by newer typing; other errors: keep previous suggestions
            }
        }
    }
}
```

**4. Deep dives**
- **Request volume**: debounce + minimum length + client cache + CDN caching for popular prefixes reduce load by an order of magnitude.
- **Race conditions**: cancellation ensures an older, slower response can't overwrite newer suggestions.
- **Offline**: show recent searches and locally indexed content (e.g. contacts, downloaded items) when offline.
- **Backend** (brief): prefix index (trie / search engine with edge n-grams), ranking by popularity and personalization, locale-aware.
- **IME languages** (Chinese/Japanese): don't query on marked (uncommitted) text.
- **Analytics**: log query → selected suggestion position to improve ranking.

```swift
@MainActor
@Observable
final class CheckoutViewModel {
    enum Phase: Equatable {
        case reviewing, paying, requiresAction(URL), confirmed(orderID: String), failed(String)
    }

    private(set) var phase: Phase = .reviewing
    private let orders: OrderAPI
    private let payments: PaymentService
    private let pendingStore: PendingCheckoutStore      // persists the idempotency key per quote

    init(orders: OrderAPI, payments: PaymentService, pendingStore: PendingCheckoutStore) {
        self.orders = orders
        self.payments = payments
        self.pendingStore = pendingStore
    }

    func placeOrder(quote: Quote) async {
        guard phase == .reviewing else { return }          // prevent double taps
        phase = .paying

        // Same key for every retry of this quote, even after an app restart
        let idempotencyKey = await pendingStore.key(for: quote.id)

        do {
            let token = try await payments.authorize(amount: quote.total, currency: quote.currency)
            let result = try await withRetry(maxAttempts: 3) {
                try await orders.create(quoteID: quote.id, paymentToken: token, idempotencyKey: idempotencyKey)
            }
            switch result {
            case .confirmed(let orderID):
                await pendingStore.clear(for: quote.id)
                phase = .confirmed(orderID: orderID)
            case .requiresAction(let url):
                phase = .requiresAction(url)                // present 3-D Secure, then poll order status
            }
        } catch {
            // Unknown outcome (e.g. timeout)? Query order status by idempotency key before allowing a new attempt
            phase = .failed("We couldn't confirm your order. We'll check its status and notify you.")
        }
    }
}
```

- **Offline**: cart browsing/editing works offline; checkout requires network and says so clearly.
- **Fraud and security**: App Attest, server-side risk checks, no secrets in the client.
- **Funnel analytics**: cart viewed → checkout started → payment authorized → order confirmed, with drop-off reasons.

```swift
    let eventID: UUID
    let name: String
    let timestamp: Date
    let properties: [String: String]

    private let continuation: AsyncStream<TrackedEvent>.Continuation
```

```swift
import CryptoKit

/// Stable across launches and platforms. Never use `hashValue`: Swift seeds it randomly per process
func bucket(for userID: String, salt: String, buckets: Int = 10_000) -> Int {
    let digest = SHA256.hash(data: Data("\(salt):\(userID)".utf8))
    let value = digest.prefix(8).reduce(UInt64(0)) { ($0 << 8) | UInt64($1) }
    return Int(value % UInt64(buckets))
}

func isInRollout(userID: String, flagKey: String, percent: Double) -> Bool {
    Double(bucket(for: userID, salt: flagKey)) < percent / 100 * 10_000
}

func variant(for userID: String, experiment: String, variants: [(name: String, weight: Int)]) -> String {
    let totalWeight = variants.reduce(0) { $0 + $1.weight }
    var point = bucket(for: userID, salt: experiment, buckets: totalWeight)
    for variant in variants {
        if point < variant.weight { return variant.name }
        point -= variant.weight
    }
    return variants.last?.name ?? "control"
}
```

```swift
final class StoryProgressController {
    private(set) var progress: Double = 0        // 0...1 for the current item
    private var elapsed: Duration = .zero
    private var timerTask: Task<Void, Never>?
    private let tick: Duration = .milliseconds(50)
    var onFinished: (() -> Void)?

    func start(duration: Duration) {
        elapsed = .zero
        progress = 0
        resume(duration: duration)
    }

    func pause() {                                // hold gesture, app backgrounded, video buffering
        timerTask?.cancel()
        timerTask = nil
    }

    func resume(duration: Duration) {
        timerTask?.cancel()
        timerTask = Task { [weak self] in
            while let self, !Task.isCancelled, self.elapsed < duration {
                try? await Task.sleep(for: self.tick)
                guard !Task.isCancelled else { return }
                self.elapsed += self.tick
                self.progress = min(1, self.elapsed / duration)
            }
            if !Task.isCancelled { self?.onFinished?() }
        }
    }
}
```

### Q117. Design a fitness app that records runs with GPS (Strava-like). `[Senior]`

**1. Requirements**
- Functional: start/pause/stop a run; live distance, pace, and route map; works with the screen locked; saves offline; uploads later; history and stats.
- Non-functional: accurate tracking, minimal battery drain, no data loss if the app crashes mid-run, privacy controls (hide start/end locations).

**2. Architecture**
```
RunView (+ Live Activity on the Lock Screen) ─▶ RunViewModel ─▶ RunRecorder
                                                                 ├── LocationStream (CLLocationUpdate.liveUpdates)
                                                                 ├── Filter (accuracy, speed outliers, smoothing)
                                                                 ├── Metrics (distance, pace, splits)
                                                                 └── RouteStore (append points incrementally to DB)
SyncService ─▶ upload completed runs (compressed polyline) when online
HealthKit (optional) ─▶ save workouts
```

**3. Recording**
```swift
import CoreLocation

@MainActor
final class RunRecorder {
    private var backgroundSession: CLBackgroundActivitySession?
    private var recordingTask: Task<Void, Never>?
    private let store: RouteStore
    private(set) var distanceMeters: Double = 0
    private var lastLocation: CLLocation?

    init(store: RouteStore) { self.store = store }

    func start(runID: UUID) {
        backgroundSession = CLBackgroundActivitySession()   // keeps updates flowing in the background (iOS 17+)
        recordingTask = Task {
            do {
                for try await update in CLLocationUpdate.liveUpdates(.fitness) {
                    guard let location = update.location,
                          location.horizontalAccuracy >= 0,
                          location.horizontalAccuracy <= 20 else { continue }   // drop inaccurate fixes

                    if let lastLocation {
                        distanceMeters += location.distance(from: lastLocation)
                    }
                    lastLocation = location
                    try? await store.append(RoutePoint(location: location), to: runID)  // crash-safe persistence
                }
            } catch {
                // location services unavailable or denied: surface to the UI
            }
        }
    }

    func stop() {
        recordingTask?.cancel()
        recordingTask = nil
        backgroundSession?.invalidate()
        backgroundSession = nil
    }
}
```

**4. Deep dives**
- **Battery**: `.fitness` activity configuration; stop updates during auto-pause; avoid map rendering while backgrounded; batch database writes.
- **Accuracy**: filter low-accuracy points and impossible speeds; smooth with a simple Kalman filter or moving average; distance from filtered points.
- **Crash safety**: points written incrementally; on relaunch, detect an unfinished run and offer to resume or save.
- **Upload**: compress the route (encoded polyline), upload with idempotency; retries in background.
- **Privacy zones**: trim route segments near home/work before upload.
- **Watch**: `HKWorkoutSession` on Apple Watch for heart rate and standalone recording, syncing via HealthKit.

### Q118. Design a push notification system end to end. `[Lead/Expert]`

**1. Requirements**
- Functional: send transactional (order shipped, new message) and marketing notifications; user preferences per category; quiet hours; localization; deep links; rich media; analytics (sent, delivered, opened).
- Non-functional: low latency for transactional pushes; high throughput for campaigns (millions of devices); no duplicates; respect rate limits and user preferences.

**2. Architecture**
```
Producers (order service, chat service, marketing tool)
      │  NotificationRequest { user_id, category, template_id, data, priority, dedupe_key }
      ▼
Notification Service
  ├── Preference check (category opt-in, quiet hours, frequency caps)
  ├── Dedupe (dedupe_key, TTL)
  ├── Template rendering + localization (user locale)
  ├── Fan-out: user → device tokens (Token Registry)
  ├── Priority queues (transactional ▶ marketing)
  ▼
APNs Provider workers (HTTP/2 connection pools, JWT auth)
  ├── handles 410 Unregistered → remove token
  └── handles 429 / 5xx → retry with backoff
      ▼
APNs ──▶ Device ──▶ Notification Service Extension (rich media, "delivered" receipt) ──▶ user taps ──▶ app routes deep link, logs "opened"
```

**3. Payload**
```json
{
  "aps": {
    "alert": { "title": "Your order has shipped", "body": "Arriving Thursday" },
    "sound": "default",
    "thread-id": "order_A1B2",
    "category": "ORDER_UPDATE",
    "mutable-content": 1,
    "interruption-level": "active"
  },
  "deeplink": "myshop://order/A1B2",
  "image_url": "https://cdn.example.com/p/123?w=600",
  "notification_id": "ntf_9f1"
}
```

**4. Deep dives**
- **Token registry**: user ↔ devices with app version, locale, notification permission status, last seen; updated on each launch; tokens unlinked on logout.
- **Delivery tracking**: APNs accepting a request ≠ device delivery. The NSE can report "delivered" (best effort), and the app logs "opened" with `notification_id`.
- **Rate limiting and fatigue**: per-user frequency caps, quiet hours (in the user's time zone), category opt-outs, global campaign throttling.
- **Collapse and expiry**: `apns-collapse-id` for superseding updates; `apns-expiration` for time-sensitive content.
- **Client**: permission priming; categories with actions (reply, track order); deep link routing; badge count from server truth; clearing notifications when content is read on another device (silent push or on open).
- **Reliability**: queues with retries and dead-letter handling; idempotent sends via dedupe keys; monitoring of send latency and failure rates.

## 17. Common Follow-Up Deep Dives

### Q119. "What happens if the user is offline for a week?" `[Senior]`
**Talking points:**
- **Reads**: cached data shown with "last updated" indicators; stale content clearly labeled where it matters (prices, availability).
- **Writes**: queued in the outbox with timestamps; some operations may become invalid (expired coupons, sold-out items). The server rejects them with clear errors, and the app explains what happened.
- **Sync on return**: delta sync from the last token; if the token expired, full resync merged carefully with unsynced local changes.
- **Auth**: refresh tokens may have expired. Keep the queued data and ask the user to log in again, then resume sync.
- **Storage**: outbox growth bounded; large media queued for Wi-Fi.

### Q120. "How does your design change for 10× more users?" `[Senior]`
**Talking points:**
- The client mostly scales naturally (each device handles one user), but the client's behavior affects backend load:
  - **Reduce request volume**: caching, conditional requests, delta sync, debouncing, batching analytics.
  - **Avoid thundering herds**: jittered retries and jittered background refresh; staggered config fetches after releases or push campaigns.
  - **CDN-friendly APIs** for public content; cursor pagination.
  - **Graceful degradation** when the backend sheds load (feature flags, cached fallbacks).
- On the backend: horizontal scaling, sharding by user/conversation, queues, caching layers (mention briefly).

### Q121. "What if the backend goes down?" `[Senior]`
**Talking points:**
- Cached content and offline-first features keep the core usable; writes queue for later.
- Circuit breakers stop hammering failing endpoints; retries with exponential backoff and jitter.
- Remote-config-driven incident banners; non-critical features disabled via kill switches.
- Launch never blocks on non-essential services (remote config, recommendations).
- Afterwards: monitor recovery, avoid synchronized retries, and review incident metrics.

### Q122. "How would you cut the app's data usage in half?" `[Senior]`
**Talking points:**
1. **Measure**: break down bytes by request type (images, video, API, analytics) using production metrics.
2. **Images/video** (usually the biggest share): device-appropriate sizes, HEIC/WebP/AVIF, lower quality on cellular, no autoplay or lower bitrates in Low Data Mode, smarter prefetching.
3. **API**: field trimming via BFF/GraphQL, gzip/brotli, delta sync, conditional requests.
4. **Analytics**: batching and compression; sampling verbose events.
5. **Caching**: improve hit rates (larger disk cache, better keys, longer TTLs for static assets).
6. Validate with an experiment and monitor engagement to ensure UX didn't degrade.

### Q123. "How do you make this work on a five-year-old iPhone?" `[Senior]`
**Talking points:**
- Profile on the oldest supported devices; set performance budgets for them.
- Adaptive quality: fewer concurrent video players, smaller images, simpler effects; `ProcessInfo.isLowPowerModeEnabled` and thermal state (`ProcessInfo.thermalState`) to reduce work.
- Memory discipline (downsampling, bounded caches) since older devices have less RAM.
- Avoid features requiring newer hardware or provide fallbacks (`#available`, capability checks).

### Q124. "How do you keep data consistent across a user's devices?" `[Lead/Expert]`
**Talking points:**
- The server is the authoritative store; each device keeps a sync token and applies changes from the server's change feed.
- Real-time hints (WebSocket events, silent pushes) trigger syncs; foreground and launch always sync.
- Conflict resolution with version numbers and a defined policy (Q42).
- Per-device read state reconciled (e.g. reading a message on iPad clears the notification on iPhone).
- Clearly define consistency expectations: eventual consistency within seconds when online.

### Q125. "How would you test this system?" `[Senior]`
**Talking points:**
- **Unit tests**: view models, sync logic, conflict resolution, state machines, mappers, retry policies (with injected clocks).
- **Integration tests**: API client with `URLProtocol` stubs; database with in-memory stores; migrations with fixture files.
- **Contract tests** against the API schema.
- **Network condition testing**: Network Link Conditioner (high latency, loss, 3G), airplane-mode toggling, background/foreground transitions, app kills mid-sync.
- **UI tests** for critical flows with a mocked backend.
- **Load/soak**: large local datasets (100k messages), long sessions for memory leaks.
- **Beta and staged rollout** with monitoring as the final safety net.

### Q126. "What would you monitor after launch?" `[Senior]`
**Talking points:**
- Crash-free rate, hangs, memory terminations (by version and device).
- Time to first content, API latency percentiles, error rates by endpoint/code.
- Feature-specific health: message delivery latency, upload success rate, sync failures, push opt-in and open rates.
- Business KPIs tied to the feature (conversion, engagement) through A/B tests.
- Alerts with thresholds during phased rollout; dashboards segmented by version, OS, region, network type.

### Q127. "What would you build in 2 weeks versus 6 months?" `[Lead/Expert]`
**Talking points:**
- **2 weeks (MVP)**: online-first with a simple cache, REST polling or basic push, minimal offline (read-only cache), basic analytics, feature-flagged release.
- **6 months**: offline-first sync with outbox and conflict handling, WebSocket real-time with gap fill, image/video pipelines, advanced observability, experimentation, performance tuning, accessibility and localization polish.
- Explain the order: what reduces risk and delivers user value first, and which decisions are hard to reverse (API contracts, data model, IDs) and deserve care even in the MVP.

### Q128. "How does the design change for iPad, Mac, watchOS, or widgets?" `[Senior]`
**Talking points:**
- **Shared core**: domain, data, and sync layers in Swift packages used by all targets; platform-specific UI.
- **iPad/Mac**: multiple windows/scenes (per-scene state), larger layouts (split views), keyboard and pointer support.
- **Widgets and Live Activities**: read from a shared App Group store; timelines refreshed after data changes; tight memory limits.
- **watchOS**: small payloads, independent networking or relay through the phone, complications; aggressive battery constraints.
- **Extensions** (share, notification service): shared Keychain access group and data container; lightweight dependencies.

## 18. Common Mistakes & Recovery

### Q129. What are the most common mistakes in mobile system design interviews? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- Jumping into solutions without clarifying requirements and scope.
- Designing a backend system and ignoring client concerns (offline, lifecycle, battery, memory, old app versions).
- No API design or data model, only boxes and arrows.
- Ignoring failure modes: no retries, idempotency, or conflict handling.
- Over-engineering (microservices, CRDTs, GraphQL federation for a simple app) without justification.
- Not stating trade-offs or alternatives.
- Running out of time before the deep dives because the requirements discussion dragged on.
- Silent thinking: not narrating reasoning for the interviewer.

### Q130. How do you know whether you're over- or under-engineering in an interview? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Key points:**
- **Under-engineering signals**: no offline story for an app that obviously needs one (chat, notes), no pagination for feeds, no handling of duplicates or retries for payments/messages.
- **Over-engineering signals**: introducing complex technology without tying it to a requirement; designing for scale the interviewer said isn't needed.
- Tie every major component to a requirement, and offer phased evolution: "For v1 I'd do X; if we see Y, we'd evolve to Z."

### Q131. What do you do if you get stuck or the interviewer challenges your design? `[Senior]`
**Key points:**
- Restate the requirement in question, think out loud, and list options with pros and cons.
- Treat challenges as collaboration: "Good point. If the user can have multiple devices, last-write-wins would lose edits; let me switch to version-based conflict detection."
- If you don't know a specific API, describe the concept and how you'd find out ("I'd use the platform's background transfer API; on iOS that's a background `URLSession`").
- Keep an eye on time; summarize where you are and propose the next topic.

## 20. Lead-Level Scenario Questions

### Q132. How do you lead a system design review for a feature spanning iOS, Android, web, and backend? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Talking points:**
- Start with a written **design doc**: problem, goals and non-goals, user flows, API contract, data model, client architecture per platform, rollout plan, metrics, risks, alternatives considered.
- Agree on the **API contract early** (schema-first with OpenAPI/GraphQL/Protobuf), including error codes, pagination, idempotency, and versioning.
- Align on shared behaviors (offline rules, conflict policy, analytics events) so platforms behave consistently.
- Review with stakeholders (product, design, security, backend, QA); record decisions and open questions; schedule follow-ups.
- Define milestones with feature flags so platforms can ship independently.

### Q133. Native, Kotlin Multiplatform, Flutter, or React Native: how would you decide? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Talking points:**
- **Criteria**: team skills and hiring, performance and UX expectations, platform API depth (widgets, Live Activities, HealthKit), code sharing goals, long-term maintenance, existing codebases.
- **Native (Swift/Kotlin)**: best platform fidelity and performance, earliest access to new APIs; costs two codebases.
- **Kotlin Multiplatform**: share business logic/networking/persistence while keeping native UI; good for teams wanting native UX with shared core logic.
- **Flutter / React Native**: share UI and logic; faster for multi-platform teams; trade-offs in platform integration, app size, and access to the newest native APIs.
- Recommend a **spike** on the hardest feature and decide with data; consider hybrid approaches (shared core, native UI).

### Q134. How would you evaluate migrating from REST to GraphQL? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Talking points:**
- **Problems to solve?** Over-fetching, too many round trips, many clients needing different shapes, slow backend iteration for screen-specific endpoints.
- **Costs**: client caching complexity (normalized caches), tooling (Apollo, code generation), server complexity (resolvers, N+1 queries, query cost limiting), loss of simple HTTP/CDN caching.
- **Alternatives**: a mobile BFF with screen-oriented REST endpoints may solve the same problems with less change.
- **Migration**: pilot one feature behind a flag, measure payload size, latency, and developer velocity, then decide; run REST and GraphQL side by side during transition.

### Q135. How would you build a mobile platform team's shared infrastructure? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Talking points:**
- Identify cross-cutting needs: networking (auth, retries, tracing), persistence, image pipeline, analytics, feature flags, design system, logging, crash reporting, CI/CD tooling.
- Build them as **well-documented modules** with clear APIs, owned by the platform team, consumed by feature teams.
- Treat feature teams as customers: gather requirements, publish roadmaps, provide migration guides and support.
- Measure success: adoption, incident reduction, build times, developer satisfaction, time to ship new features.

### Q136. How do you plan a large feature (e.g. offline mode for an existing app) with manageable risk? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Talking points:**
- **Phase 1**: read-only offline cache for top screens (high value, low risk).
- **Phase 2**: offline writes for simple, low-conflict actions (likes, bookmarks) via an outbox.
- **Phase 3**: complex offline edits with conflict resolution for core entities.
- Each phase behind feature flags, with metrics (sync failures, conflicts, data loss reports) and rollback plans.
- Early investment in irreversible decisions (client-generated IDs, versioned entities, change feed APIs); later phases build on them.

### Q137. A release causes crashes on launch for 5% of users. What do you do? `[Lead/Expert]`
**Talking points:**
1. **Contain**: pause the phased rollout; disable the suspected feature via kill switch if the crash path is flag-controlled; post an in-app/status message if possible.
2. **Diagnose**: crash reports by device/OS/version; recent changes (migrations, SDK updates, feature flags); reproduce with affected configurations.
3. **Fix**: hotfix build with expedited App Review if necessary; for migration crashes, add recovery paths rather than `fatalError`.
4. **Communicate**: stakeholders, support teams, and affected users.
5. **Prevent**: post-mortem with actions (launch-crash tests, migration fixtures in CI, safer rollout thresholds, launch-path code reviews).

### Q138. How do you mentor engineers to get better at system design? `[Senior]`
**Talking points:**
- Run regular **design reviews** where engineers present designs for real features; give feedback on reasoning and trade-offs, not just conclusions.
- Share a **design doc template** and examples of good docs.
- Pair on design for complex features; encourage writing down assumptions and alternatives.
- Mock system design sessions using prompts like Section 19, with constructive debriefs.
- Encourage reading post-mortems and architecture write-ups from other teams and companies.

### Final Tips
- **Structure beats brilliance**: clarify requirements, draw the architecture, define the API and data model, then go deep. Interviewers reward a clear, well-paced process.
- **Mobile-specific depth is your differentiator**: offline and sync (Q39–Q46), real-time with gap fill (Q47–Q52), push realities (Q53–Q58), background limits (Q59–Q62), media pipelines (Q63–Q70), and resource constraints (Q80–Q87).
- **Always cover failure modes**: retries with jitter, idempotency keys, duplicate handling, app termination, old app versions, and backend outages.
- **Rehearse 4–5 designs end to end** (feed, chat, image loader, offline notes, and one domain close to the job you're applying for), timing yourself to finish requirements and architecture within the first 15–20 minutes.
- For **Lead roles**, add rollout plans, metrics, cross-team API contracts, phased delivery, and risk management to every design.
